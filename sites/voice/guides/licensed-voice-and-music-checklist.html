Licensed voice and music checklist for commercial audio

Check the license scope, media, territory, duration, edits, and platform before publishing.

Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Keep the license with the project, confirm the permitted media and territory, check whether editing and synthetic use are allowed, and verify that the final placement matches the license.

The problem

A voice or music track can be legal for one use and illegal for another. Teams often download an asset and assume the license covers advertising, editing, or unlimited distribution.

Match the license to the actual use

A license for a personal video may not cover a paid ad. A music license for online use may not cover broadcast. A voice sample may be limited to one project. Read the permitted media, audience, territory, and duration.

Check editing and derivative rights

Confirm whether you can cut, pitch, process, remix, loop, or combine the asset with synthetic generation. Some licenses prohibit altering the voice or using it to train a model. Keep the exact asset and license version.

Verify music and sound-effect rights separately

A voice license does not cover the background music. A stock music license may not cover a brand campaign. Check each layer of the mix, including sound effects and samples embedded in the track.

Record attribution and disclosure requirements

Some licenses require credit, a link, or a disclosure. Others prohibit implying endorsement. Put the required credit in the deliverable and check that the platform supports it.

Archive the final asset and proof

Store the license, receipt, source URL, download date, final edit, and publishing destinations. If a claim or takedown arrives, you need to show that the exact use was permitted.

Checklist

  • Does the license cover the intended media and territory?
  • Are editing and synthetic-use rights clear?
  • Are music and sound effects licensed separately?
  • Are credit or disclosure requirements met?
  • Is the license archived with the final asset?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming a free download is free for commercial use.
  • Using a personal-use track in a paid ad.
  • Editing a voice sample beyond the license scope.
  • Failing to keep the receipt and license version.

FAQ

Can I use a licensed voice to train a model?

Only if the license explicitly permits model training or synthetic use. A standard voiceover license usually does not.

What if the license changes after I download?

Keep the version that applied at download time and check whether the provider reserves the right to change terms. Archive the receipt and terms.

Do I need a lawyer for every license?

Not for routine low-risk assets, but high-value campaigns, broad rights, and synthetic voice use deserve professional review.
