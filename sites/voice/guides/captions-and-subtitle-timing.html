Captions and subtitle timing: a QC workflow for spoken audio

Balance reading speed, line breaks, sync, speaker changes, and visual obstruction.

Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Create a transcript, segment it by meaning and timing, check characters per second, keep line breaks natural, and review the final captions in the actual player.

The problem

A transcript is not a subtitle. Captions must be timed, segmented, and checked against the performance while remaining readable on a phone.

Segment by meaning, not character count alone

A subtitle should not split a name, a negation, or a two-word phrase that only makes sense together. Start from sentence meaning, then adjust the timing and length so the viewer can read comfortably.

Check reading speed on the smallest screen

A dense caption may fit on a desktop but fail on a phone. Measure characters per second and review line length. Shorten redundant wording rather than shrinking the font or flashing text.

Identify speakers and non-speech audio

When several people speak, use speaker labels consistently. Include meaningful non-speech audio such as [laughs] only when it affects understanding. Check that captions match the final mix and not an earlier script.

Keep captions clear of the visual subject

Check the safe area on the actual frame. Captions should not cover faces, product names, charts, or calls to action. If the video has burned-in text, the caption placement may need a separate layout.

Review the exported captions in the player

Import the final caption file into the destination player and watch the whole piece. Check sync drift, line breaks, gaps, overlaps, punctuation, and accessibility settings. A correct text file can still display badly.

Checklist

  • Are captions segmented by meaning?
  • Is reading speed comfortable on a phone?
  • Are speaker changes and meaningful sounds labeled?
  • Do captions avoid important visuals?
  • Did I watch the final captions in the target player?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Publishing the raw transcript without timing.
  • Using character count as the only segmentation rule.
  • Ignoring speaker labels in interviews.
  • Checking captions only in the editing application.

FAQ

What is a good caption reading speed?

It depends on the audience and platform, but lower reading speeds generally improve comprehension. Test on a phone and follow the destination platform’s guidance.

Should I caption sound effects?

Caption meaningful sounds that affect understanding. Do not clutter the screen with every background noise.

Can auto-captions be published directly?

Only after editing names, punctuation, speaker labels, timing, and sync. Auto-captions are a draft, not a finished accessibility deliverable.
