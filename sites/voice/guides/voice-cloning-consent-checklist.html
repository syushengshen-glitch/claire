Voice cloning consent checklist: permission, scope, and revocation

Document who approved the voice, what it may be used for, where it can appear, and when permission ends.

Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Obtain explicit written consent for cloning, define the allowed uses and markets, set an expiry or review date, record compensation, provide a revocation path, and never use the voice outside the agreed scope.

The problem

A recorded sample is not the same as permission to create a synthetic likeness. Voice cloning raises identity, privacy, employment, and fraud risks that a generic release may not cover.

Separate recording consent from cloning consent

Consent to record a voiceover for one project does not automatically permit training a synthetic voice. The consent should explicitly mention cloning, model training, synthesis, and the intended outputs. Use plain language and a separate signature if the risk is material.

Define the exact allowed use

List the projects, brands, channels, languages, duration, and territories where the synthetic voice may appear. State whether political, adult, medical, financial, or sensitive content is excluded. If the scope is broad, explain why and compensate accordingly.

Document compensation and ownership

Clarify whether the person is paid a one-time fee, a usage fee, a royalty, or nothing. State who owns the model and generated outputs, and what happens if the relationship ends. Do not hide a broad rights grab inside a recording release.

Provide review and revocation

Give the voice owner a way to request corrections, pause new uses, or revoke consent where the law and contract allow. Define how existing published content will be handled. A revocation path reduces the risk of an indefinite, uncontrolled synthetic identity.

Secure the model and generated files

Restrict who can access the voice model, samples, and generation tools. Log generations, review outputs, and remove access when the project ends. A cloned voice is sensitive identity data, not just another media asset.

Checklist

  • Does the consent explicitly mention cloning and model training?
  • Are allowed and excluded uses listed?
  • Are territory, duration, and compensation clear?
  • Is there a review or revocation path?
  • Is access to the model and outputs controlled?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Treating a normal voiceover release as cloning consent.
  • Using an open-ended “all media forever” clause without discussion.
  • Leaving the model accessible to the whole organization indefinitely.
  • Assuming a synthetic voice cannot harm the person it resembles.

FAQ

Is voice cloning always illegal?

No, but the law varies by jurisdiction and use case. Identity, privacy, consumer protection, employment, and platform rules can all apply. Obtain informed consent and legal review for commercial use.

Can I clone my own voice?

Usually with proper security and disclosure, but check the platform terms and the rights of any people or copyrighted material in the recording.

What if the voice owner wants to revoke later?

Follow the agreed revocation process, stop new generations, review existing uses, and document the action. The contract and applicable law determine what is required.
