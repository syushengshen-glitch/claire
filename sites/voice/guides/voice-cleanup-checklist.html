Voice cleanup checklist: fix the recording before reaching for AI

A practical order for noise, clicks, plosives, breaths, sibilance, and room problems.

Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Fix the room and gain first, edit obvious clicks and mistakes, then use light noise reduction, de-essing, EQ, and compression only where needed. Check on phone speakers and headphones.

The problem

Teams often stack noise reduction, de-essing, and AI enhancement until the voice sounds metallic. The original recording, edit decisions, and monitoring environment determine most of the quality.

Start with the source and the room

Reflections, fan noise, and a poor microphone position cannot be fully repaired later. Record a 30-second room tone and a test sentence. If the room is the problem, move the microphone, add soft furnishings, or choose a quieter space before processing.

Set gain and check for clipping

Leave headroom and avoid clipping at the interface. A normalized file is not the same as a well-recorded file. Check the waveform and listen for distortion on loud consonants and emotional peaks.

Edit the performance before the tone

Remove false starts, long pauses, mouth clicks, and obvious mistakes. Keep breaths that sound natural unless they distract. Heavy-handed removal can make the voice sound unnatural and create audible gaps.

Use processing in small, deliberate steps

High-pass filter rumble, use gentle noise reduction, control sibilance, then shape tone with EQ and compression. A/B every change at matched loudness. If the voice becomes thinner or metallic, back off.

Check the final mix in the real playback context

Listen on a phone speaker, laptop, headphones, and the destination platform. Check intelligibility under music, level consistency across speakers, captions, and the final loudness target. Export a test file and play it back through the actual app.

Example

Cleanup order

Choose a quiet room and set input gain with headroom.

Record room tone and a test sentence.

Edit mistakes, clicks, and distracting breaths.

Apply gentle noise reduction, de-essing, EQ, and compression.

Check phone, laptop, headphones, and final platform loudness.

Checklist

  • Did I fix the room and microphone position first?
  • Is there no clipping or interface distortion?
  • Are mistakes and clicks edited before processing?
  • Did I A/B every processor at matched loudness?
  • Did I check the final file on phone speakers?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using heavy noise reduction to hide a bad room.
  • Removing every breath and making speech sound robotic.
  • Applying EQ and compression without a reference.
  • Exporting without checking the destination platform.

FAQ

How much noise reduction is safe?

Use the least amount that solves the audible problem. If the voice starts sounding hollow or metallic, reduce the strength or improve the source.

Should I remove all breaths?

No. Natural breaths help speech sound human. Remove only the ones that distract, are unusually loud, or interrupt the intended pacing.

Is AI voice enhancement a replacement for editing?

No. It can help with residual noise or clarity, but it cannot recover a clipped recording or remove poor edit decisions.
