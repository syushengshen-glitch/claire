Loudness for podcast and video: LUFS, peaks, and platform delivery

A plain-language loudness workflow that avoids quiet exports, clipped peaks, and inconsistent episodes.

Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Choose the platform target, measure integrated loudness and true peaks, keep dynamics appropriate for the format, and test the final export in the app where people will listen.

The problem

A mix can sound fine in the editor and quiet on a phone or loud on a streaming platform. Loudness is a relationship between average level, peaks, and the playback target.

Separate loudness from peak level

Integrated loudness describes the average perceived level over time; true peak catches inter-sample peaks that can clip after encoding. A track can be loud enough on average and still clip at the peaks. Measure both.

Use a target suited to the format

Podcasts, social video, training modules, and broadcast have different norms and platform guidance. Pick a target, document it, and keep a consistent internal standard. Do not use one master for every platform without checking the destination.

Preserve useful dynamics

Compression can make speech easier to hear in noisy environments, but too much reduces emotional range and creates fatigue. A calm narration and a high-energy ad need different movement, even if both target the same average loudness.

Check across speakers and devices

Listen on phone speakers, laptop speakers, and headphones. Check the quietest important word and the loudest sound effect. If the voice disappears under music, adjust the mix rather than simply raising the master.

Measure the final encoded file

Encoding can change peaks. Export the final format, re-import it, and measure again. Keep a QC note with the integrated loudness, true peak, target platform, and date.

Checklist

  • Did I choose a platform-specific loudness target?
  • Did I measure integrated loudness and true peak?
  • Does the mix preserve useful dynamics?
  • Does speech remain intelligible under music?
  • Did I measure the final encoded file?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Normalizing peaks instead of measuring loudness.
  • Using one master for every platform.
  • Over-compressing until the voice is fatiguing.
  • Checking only in headphones.

FAQ

What LUFS should I use?

It depends on the platform and format. Use the platform’s current delivery guidance, document the target, and keep it consistent within a series.

Why does my export clip after encoding?

Lossy encoding can raise peaks. Leave headroom, control true peaks, and test the final encoded file rather than the editing project.

Should I master every episode identically?

Use a standard, but adjust for content. A quiet interview and a high-energy promo may need different dynamic treatment even with the same target.
