How to build a pronunciation guide for names, brands, and acronyms

Turn a pronunciation disagreement into a documented, testable delivery decision.

Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Create one pronunciation list with the written form, phonetic cue, language, preferred recording, and owner. Approve the list before the full session and store it with the project.

The problem

A client says the brand name one way, the writer says another, and the voice actor guesses. The final file sounds polished but still says the most important word incorrectly.

List every word that can be said differently

Include product names, people, places, acronyms, numbers, currencies, dates, and foreign terms. Add the language variety and the intended audience. If the same word has different pronunciations in different markets, make separate entries.

Use a cue the voice actor can repeat

A dictionary phonetic spelling may be enough, but a short reference recording is often clearer. Include syllable stress, vowel sound, and any silent letters. Avoid vague instructions such as “say it naturally” when the client has a required pronunciation.

Assign one approval owner

The producer should collect input, but one person must approve the final pronunciation. Otherwise the voice actor receives conflicting notes and the most important name becomes a committee decision.

Test the word inside a real sentence

A name can be correct in isolation but awkward at the pace of the script. Put it into the actual line, record it, and listen on a phone speaker. Check that the stress does not distort the surrounding grammar.

Store the approved guide with the project

The guide should travel with the script, glossary, and rights notes. Future episodes, translations, and pickups should use the same reference rather than starting the debate again.

Example

Pronunciation entry

Written: Kalla

Phonetic cue: KAH-lah (first syllable stressed).

Reference: client recording 0:03–0:05.

Owner: brand lead.

Note: do not pronounce it like “caller.”

Checklist

  • Are all ambiguous words listed?
  • Is there a repeatable phonetic or audio cue?
  • Does one owner approve the final pronunciation?
  • Was the word tested inside a real sentence?
  • Is the guide stored with the project?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Relying on the writer’s guess.
  • Approving a pronunciation after the full record.
  • Using one entry for several language markets.
  • Forgetting to test the word at the final pace.

FAQ

Should I use IPA?

IPA can be precise, but not every voice actor or client reads it. Pair it with a reference recording and a plain-language cue.

What if the client is unsure?

Ask for a recording, not another written explanation. If the client still cannot decide, the producer should choose an interim standard and mark it for future review.

How many pickups should I budget?

For a pronunciation-heavy script, budget a short pickup window. It is cheaper than re-recording the full piece.
