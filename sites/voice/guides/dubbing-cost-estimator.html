How to estimate dubbing cost without forgetting the review work

Budget translation, casting, recording, direction, editing, mixing, QC, and project management separately.

Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Estimate each language separately: source preparation, translation, adaptation, voice recording, direction, editing and mixing, QC, and project management. Add a revision buffer and test one short scene before quoting the full series.

The problem

A per-minute voice quote can look affordable until translation, adaptation, casting, editing, and multilingual QC are added. The real budget is the total work required to produce an approved version.

Separate translation from adaptation

A literal translation is not a dubbing script. The adaptation must fit the timing, character voice, and cultural context. Budget for a separate adaptation or transcreation pass, especially for humor, jargon, and tight timing.

Budget casting and direction

Each language may need a different voice, audition, and director. A voice that fits the original may not fit the localized version. Include rehearsal and pickup time, not only the final recorded minutes.

Include editing, mixing, and QC

Dubbing requires comping, noise reduction, music and effects balancing, loudness matching, subtitle or caption checks, and native-language review. These steps are part of the deliverable, not overhead.

Estimate by approved minutes, not raw footage

A 30-minute interview may require cutting to 18 minutes before dubbing. Quote the approved runtime plus a revision allowance. Raw footage can hide significant editing and adaptation work.

Run a paid pilot scene

Produce one short scene before quoting the series. Measure actual time for adaptation, recording, QC, and revisions. The pilot reveals whether the budget and schedule are realistic.

Example

Per-language budget fields

Approved runtime: 18 minutes.

Translation and adaptation: 1.5 days.

Casting and audition: 3 candidates.

Recording and direction: 3 hours.

Editing, mixing, QC: 1 day.

Revision buffer: 15%.

Checklist

  • Is the runtime based on the approved cut?
  • Are translation and adaptation separate line items?
  • Are casting, direction, and pickups included?
  • Are editing, mixing, and native QC included?
  • Is there a revision buffer?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Quoting only voice recording minutes.
  • Using raw footage instead of the approved runtime.
  • Skipping native-language QC.
  • Forgetting music, captions, and loudness work.

FAQ

How much does dubbing cost per minute?

It varies too widely to give one number. Market, language, voice, rights, adaptation, and QC change the total. Use a line-item estimate rather than a single rate.

Can AI dubbing lower the cost?

It can reduce some recording time, but translation, adaptation, voice rights, pronunciation, and QC remain. A lower recording cost does not remove the other work.

What causes the biggest overruns?

Late script changes, poor source audio, unclear pronunciation, and insufficient native QC are common causes. A pilot scene helps expose them early.
