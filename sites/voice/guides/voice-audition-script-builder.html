The voice audition script that reveals range and pronunciation

A six-beat audition that tests clarity, warmth, authority, pacing, names, and emotional control.

Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Build a short audition with six beats: neutral explanation, warm welcome, firm instruction, technical term, emotional turn, and closing call to action. Include real names and numbers.

The problem

A generic paragraph reveals whether a voice sounds pleasant, but not whether it can carry the actual script, pronounce the brand names, or shift tone without losing control.

Make every line test a production decision

The audition should answer questions you will face in the edit: Can the voice stay clear at speed? Does it sound credible when giving a warning? Can it soften without becoming childish? Can it pronounce the product name consistently? Each line should have a purpose.

Include the hard words early

Put the product name, acronym, number, currency, date, and regional term in the first half of the script. If the voice struggles there, the rest of the performance is less useful. Ask for the pronunciation you expect and let the actor offer an alternative if the brief is wrong.

Test a controlled emotional shift

Write one sentence that moves from neutral information to a cautious warning. Listen for whether the shift feels motivated or performed. A strong audition shows restraint; not every line needs to be pushed.

Ask for two paces of the same passage

A normal read and a 15% faster read reveal whether clarity survives compression. Video and social teams often need a shorter cut. If the voice only works at one speed, the editing options are limited.

Score consistency and editability

Listen for consistent volume, breath control, mouth noise, and ending consonants. Ask whether the delivery can be cut into smaller segments without sounding stitched together. A beautiful take that cannot be edited is not a production-ready take.

Example

Six-beat audition

Neutral: “The new dashboard gives your team one place to review open requests.”

Warm: “Welcome back — let’s pick up where we left off.”

Firm: “Do not share the access code outside your organization.”

Technical: “The export uses AES-256 encryption and expires after 24 hours.”

Emotional turn: “If a client name appears in the wrong report, stop and check the source before sending.”

Close: “Start with one workflow today.”

Checklist

  • Does every line test a real production decision?
  • Are hard names and numbers included?
  • Is there one controlled emotional shift?
  • Did I ask for a faster version?
  • Can the delivery be edited into short segments?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using a generic paragraph with no brand-specific words.
  • Asking for “energy” without defining the context.
  • Testing only one pace.
  • Ignoring mouth noise, breaths, and ending consonants.

FAQ

How long should an audition be?

Sixty to ninety seconds is usually enough to test range, pronunciation, and editability without asking for unpaid full production.

Should I provide direction in the audition?

Yes. A short direction note helps compare how each voice responds to feedback, not just how it sounds alone.

Can I use the audition recording in the final project?

Only if the voice provider has agreed to that use and the rights are documented. An audition is not automatically a production license.
