Editorial method

How we decide what to publish, what to verify and what to remove.

1. Start with a real decision

A page should help a reader choose a workflow, check a resume, prepare for an interview or reduce a privacy risk. Pages that exist only to repeat a keyword or link to a vendor are not published.

2. Use primary sources

Product features, plan limits and prices are checked against the vendor's official pages. We link to the source and record the review date. When a feature cannot be verified, we describe it as a limit to verify rather than filling the gap with a guess.

3. Separate evidence from judgment

Documented product behavior is evidence. Advice about who should use the product is editorial judgment. We try to make the distinction obvious so readers can evaluate both.

4. Keep the human review step

AI tools may help with outlining, comparison or copy editing. They do not create experience. Every factual claim in a resume, interview answer or review must be traceable to a source or a real user-provided example.

5. Review dates and corrections

Pricing is checked when a page is reviewed and may change after publication. Corrections update the page and review date. Pages that no longer add a distinct answer are consolidated, removed or redirected instead of being kept as thin variants.

6. Commercial independence

Advertising is labeled. Affiliate relationships, if added, will be disclosed before the first affected link and will not determine the stated strengths or limitations of a tool. See the advertising disclosure.

7. Privacy and sensitive data

Guides that mention uploading resumes or interview material explain redaction and vendor-review steps. We do not ask readers to submit confidential employer information or another person's personal data.
