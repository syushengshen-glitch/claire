Rezi vs Teal: which resume workflow fits your job search?

One is resume-first; the other is a job-search workspace. The right choice depends on how you work.

By CareerSignal Lab Editorial Team · Reviewed November 13, 2026 · 9 min read · Advertising is labeled separately

Rezi and Teal both help with resumes, but they solve different parts of the job-search problem. Rezi is closer to a focused resume editor. Teal is closer to a job-search operating system.

The comparison below uses the same questions for both products: what does the user need to produce, what gets stored, how much control does the user have, what does the free plan allow, and what happens when the application volume increases? Pricing and public feature descriptions were checked on November 13, 2026.

1. Resume control and formatting

Rezi feels more like a dedicated resume product. Its public plan page highlights AI writing, editing, templates, resume scoring and keyword targeting. If your main pain is a resume that looks unstructured or reads like a list of duties, a guided builder can reduce formatting decisions and keep the document consistent.

Teal also provides resume creation and templates, but its value is broader. The builder is connected to a job-search workspace rather than existing as an isolated document. That is helpful when the same candidate needs several versions, but it also means more setup before the first useful output.

Practical test: create the same two-page resume in each tool using a redacted role. Count the time until the first acceptable export, then count the corrections needed after a human review. A polished template is not useful if it hides missing evidence or makes the document harder to edit.

2. Tracking multiple applications

Teal has a clear advantage when applications accumulate. Saving a role, recording the status, keeping notes and maintaining resume versions in one place reduces duplicate work. The tracking workflow is especially useful for career changers who are testing several role families at once.

Rezi's public positioning is more focused on the resume and interview-preparation experience. That focus can be a benefit: fewer fields, less maintenance and a shorter path from blank page to document. It is less attractive if you want the application pipeline itself to live in the same product.

3. Keyword feedback and ATS claims

Both products provide feedback intended to improve relevance to a posting. Neither should be treated as a guarantee of how a particular employer's ATS will parse or rank a resume. ATS systems vary; job descriptions are often incomplete; and a high match score does not prove that the candidate has the required experience.

Use keyword feedback in this order: first identify words that describe real work you have done; then decide whether the resume states that evidence clearly; finally check whether the wording is natural. Do not paste a keyword list into a summary, and do not add a tool merely because the scanning interface recommends it.

4. Price and export limits

Rezi's current public pricing lists a free plan, a $29 per month Pro plan and a $149 lifetime option, with plan details subject to change. Teal's current public pricing lists a Free Forever plan and a premium plan at $13 per week, alongside different limits for templates, matching and analysis.

Price is only one part of the comparison. Check whether the exact export you need is included, whether watermarks or download limits apply, and whether a subscription can be cancelled easily. If you only need one application, a short free trial may be enough. If you expect a long search, calculate the total cost rather than comparing monthly headline prices.

5. Data and privacy

A resume contains contact details, employment history, education and sometimes sensitive career context. Before uploading it, review the vendor's privacy policy, retention settings and account controls. Redact information that is not needed for the test, such as a home address, personal phone number, reference contacts or internal employer details.

Both tools are third-party services. Keep a local master copy and export a clean final file after each application. Do not let the only usable version of your resume live inside a subscription you may cancel.

Choose Rezi if...

  • You want a resume-first product with guided structure.
  • You are applying to a smaller set of related roles.
  • You want a straightforward path from editing to PDF export.
  • You do not need a full application tracker.

Choose Teal if...

  • You are managing many applications and need a tracker.
  • You want resume versions and application notes in one workflow.
  • You expect to compare several role families or locations.
  • You are willing to maintain the system so it remains useful.

A 45-minute comparison test

  1. Use a redacted one-page resume and one representative job description.
  2. Create the same role in both tools; do not let either tool rewrite your evidence automatically.
  3. Ask each tool for a gap analysis and note which recommendations are supported by facts.
  4. Export the result and check formatting, dates, links and page count.
  5. Record setup time, correction time, export limits, price and privacy friction in a simple table.

Verdict

Choose Rezi when the resume document is the center of the problem. Choose Teal when the job search itself needs structure and the resume is one part of a larger workflow. If both fit, start with the free option that requires less setup and only pay when the workflow has proved useful.
