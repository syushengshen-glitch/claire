Rezi review: a focused AI resume builder

A guided resume editor for candidates who want structure and faster tailoring.

By CareerSignal Lab Editorial Team · Reviewed November 13, 2026 · 8 min read

Rezi is best understood as a guided resume editor, not a complete job-search operating system. It is aimed at the moment when you have experience to communicate but need structure, cleaner bullets and a more deliberate match to a posting.

Best fit. Job seekers who want a resume-first tool with AI writing, keyword targeting and a structured editor. It is less compelling if you mainly need an application tracker.

What Rezi does well

Rezi's public product pages describe AI resume writing, resume editing, keyword scanning, ATS-oriented templates and a resume score. The combination reduces the blank-page problem. You can start with your real experience, use the editor to tighten the language and compare the result against a specific job description.

The most useful feature is the feedback loop, not the score itself. A score can flag missing language or structural issues, but it cannot know whether you actually performed the work. Treat every recommendation as a prompt to verify the evidence. If a bullet becomes more impressive but less true, reject it.

Pricing and export limits

The public plan page checked on November 13, 2026 lists a free plan with one resume, one AI interview and three PDF downloads, a Pro plan at $29 per month, and a lifetime option at $149. Plan details can change, so confirm the current price and export rules before paying.

For a single application, the free tier may be enough to evaluate the editor. For a long search, compare the monthly cost with the number of resumes and exports you realistically need. A polished first draft is not useful if the export you need is locked behind a plan you do not want.

Strengths

  • Guided resume structure with AI writing and editing tools.
  • Keyword targeting and resume scoring give you a concrete review loop.
  • A resume-first interface keeps the task focused.
  • Useful for candidates who want more direction than a blank document.

Limits to verify

  • AI suggestions can overstate ownership, seniority or results.
  • ATS scores are not a universal prediction of employer behavior.
  • Templates can make different candidates look interchangeable if the content is generic.
  • Free exports and advanced features are limited; check before subscribing.

A 20-minute useful test

  1. Use a redacted one-page resume and one real job description.
  2. Run the comparison and record every suggested keyword.
  3. Mark each suggestion as proven, related or unsupported.
  4. Rewrite only the proven and related items using your own evidence.
  5. Export the file and check dates, links, page count and PDF text selection.

Verdict

Rezi is a strong starting point when the resume document is the main bottleneck. It is not a substitute for evidence, and it is not the best choice if you need a full application tracker. Use it to improve structure and wording, then keep the final factual review human.
