Teal review: resume builder plus job tracker

A job-search workspace for candidates managing several applications and resume versions.

By CareerSignal Lab Editorial Team · Reviewed November 13, 2026 · 8 min read

Teal is a job-search workspace with a resume builder inside it. Its value is strongest when you are managing several applications and need resume versions, saved roles, notes and status information in one place.

Best fit. Candidates running a structured search across multiple roles or locations, especially people who want a tracker and resume tailoring in the same workflow.

What Teal does well

Teal's public product pages describe resume creation, job tracking, keyword matching, saved jobs and application-stage guidance. The product is designed around the full search rather than a single document. That is useful when the same candidate needs different versions of a resume and a record of what was sent where.

The tracking layer becomes valuable only if you use it consistently. Record the role, source, date, resume version, response and next action. Otherwise the workspace becomes another inbox. The best output is not a large dashboard; it is a shorter, clearer list of applications that need attention.

Pricing and feature limits

The public Teal pricing page checked on November 13, 2026 lists a Free Forever plan and a Teal+ plan at $13 per week. The page also distinguishes limits for resume templates, keyword matching and analysis. Confirm the current plan details and cancellation terms before subscribing.

Strengths

  • Combines resume tailoring with job tracking and application notes.
  • Useful for managing several versions of a resume.
  • Supports a repeatable search workflow rather than a one-off document.
  • Makes it easier to see which applications need follow-up.

Limits to verify

  • The tracker only works if you maintain it.
  • Keyword matching does not prove candidate quality or employer response.
  • Some templates and analyses are limited by plan.
  • You should review privacy, retention and export controls before uploading a full resume.

A useful Teal test

  1. Create a free account and add one redacted resume and one job description.
  2. Save the role and record its source and date.
  3. Use the keyword feedback to identify supported and unsupported requirements.
  4. Create a second version with only factual changes.
  5. Check the export, then decide whether the tracking workflow saves enough time to justify the plan.

Verdict

Teal is the better choice when the search itself needs structure. It is not necessary for a single application, and it cannot replace a human review of the evidence. If you are managing many roles and will maintain the tracker, the combined workspace can reduce friction.
