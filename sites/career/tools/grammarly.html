Grammarly for resumes: what it can and cannot fix

A useful final-pass editor, not a replacement for job-specific resume strategy.

By CareerSignal Lab Editorial Team · Reviewed November 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Grammarly is best used at the end of the resume process. It can make a document cleaner, more consistent and easier to read. It cannot decide whether the resume targets the right role, whether a claim is defensible or whether an employer's ATS will parse the file correctly.

Best fit. Candidates who already have a targeted, factual resume and want a final pass for grammar, clarity and tone.

What it does well

Grammarly's core strength is sentence-level editing: spelling, grammar, punctuation, clarity, tone and consistency. On a resume, that means removing awkward phrases, repeated words and inconsistent capitalization. It can also help a cover letter or outreach message sound more direct.

Do not accept every suggestion. Resume language is compact and sometimes intentionally omits articles. A grammar tool may flag a valid bullet or rewrite it into a longer sentence. Review each change in context and keep the version that is clear and truthful.

Plans and access

Grammarly currently offers a free plan and paid individual plans, with different feature limits and regional pricing. Check the plan page for your country before subscribing. A free grammar check may be enough if your main need is a final proofread.

A short resume editing workflow

  1. Finish the job-specific content and factual review first.
  2. Run spelling and grammar checks without accepting bulk rewrites.
  3. Check capitalization of job titles, tools and certifications.
  4. Read the document aloud after the edits.
  5. Export and inspect the final PDF or DOCX.

Limits to verify

  • It does not optimize your resume for a specific job description.
  • It may suggest a more verbose phrase for a compact bullet.
  • It cannot verify facts, dates or credentials.
  • Paid features and regional prices vary.

Verdict

Grammarly is a useful last-mile editor, especially for candidates whose first language is not English. It is not a career strategy tool. Use it after the evidence and targeting work is done, and keep the final judgment human.
