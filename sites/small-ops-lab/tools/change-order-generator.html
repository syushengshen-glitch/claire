Scope-change order generator

Turn an awkward request into a documented decision with a clear price and timeline.

This template supports the guide How to invoice a scope change without losing the client. Use it as a working draft, then adapt the wording and fields to your own contracts, client process, and jurisdiction.

How to use this page

  1. Open the source guide and read the context first.
  2. Copy the template or run the calculator with one real, non-sensitive example.
  3. Check the output against the checklist below.
  4. Save the final version in your own document or project tool.

Checklist

  • Did I pause before doing the extra work?
  • Did I quote the original scope accurately?
  • Did I describe the impact in time, cost, and risk?
  • Did I offer a real alternative?
  • Do I have written approval before starting?

Example wording

Change-order email

Thanks for the request. The current quote covers five pages and two revision rounds. Adding a sixth page with a booking form requires about six extra hours and moves the launch by three business days. Option A: add it for $900 and move the launch to October 24. Option B: keep the current scope and plan the booking page for phase two. Reply with A or B and I will update the schedule.

Do not skip

  • Absorbing the change to avoid conflict.
  • Saying no without offering an alternative.
  • Giving a verbal estimate that the client treats as final.
  • Starting the work before the change order is approved.
Scope note

This page is an operational template, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Review the final wording with a qualified professional where the risk is material.
