Payment-terms builder

Set expectations before the first invoice, not after the first missed payment.

This template supports the guide How to write a late-payment policy clients actually understand. Use it as a working draft, then adapt the wording and fields to your own contracts, client process, and jurisdiction.

How to use this page

  1. Open the source guide and read the context first.
  2. Copy the template or run the calculator with one real, non-sensitive example.
  3. Check the output against the checklist below.
  4. Save the final version in your own document or project tool.

Checklist

  • Is the due date and grace period exact?
  • Is the consequence one you will actually apply?
  • Is there a clear billing contact?
  • Are disputes handled separately from delays?
  • Does the quote, invoice, and contract use the same wording?

Example wording

Plain-language policy block

Invoices are due within 14 calendar days. If payment is not received after a 3-day grace period, new work may pause until the account is current. If a line item is disputed, contact billing@example.com within 5 days so we can review it while the undisputed balance is paid.

Do not skip

  • Writing a punitive policy you are afraid to enforce.
  • Changing the terms after the first missed payment.
  • Hiding the policy in the last paragraph of a contract.
  • Treating a legitimate dispute as a late payment.
Scope note

This page is an operational template, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Review the final wording with a qualified professional where the risk is material.
