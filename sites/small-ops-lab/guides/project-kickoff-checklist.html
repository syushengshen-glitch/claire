Project kickoff checklist: 18 items to confirm before work starts

Set the goal, owners, inputs, and decisions before the first hour of production.

Reviewed October 12, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Run a 45-minute kickoff that confirms the outcome, scope, owners, inputs, milestones, communication rhythm, and change process. Send the written summary the same day.

The problem

Projects rarely fail because nobody knows how to do the work. They fail because the goal, owner, inputs, or decision path was never confirmed.

Confirm the outcome in one sentence

Ask the client to state what success looks like and who will judge it. Write the answer in their words. If two stakeholders describe different outcomes, resolve that before production; otherwise you will build two projects.

Name every owner and input

List who approves the direction, who provides content or access, who answers technical questions, and who signs off on completion. For each input, add a due date. An unowned input is a future delay.

Map the milestones and the decision gates

Define the sequence of visible milestones and the decision required at each one. A milestone is not “work continues”; it is a deliverable that can be approved, rejected, or revised.

Agree on the communication rhythm

Decide where updates live, how often they are sent, who receives them, and what counts as urgent. A weekly written update plus a shared task board prevents most surprise meetings.

Restate scope, change control, and next steps

Close by repeating what is included, what is excluded, how changes are approved, and the first three tasks with owners and dates. Send the summary within one business day and ask for corrections.

Example

Kickoff summary skeleton

Outcome: a five-page site that can take bookings without phone tag.

Owners: Maya approves design, Sam provides final copy, Alex owns build.

Milestones: sitemap approved Oct 8; design direction Oct 12; build complete Oct 20.

Rhythm: written update every Friday; urgent issues by email with URGENT in the subject.

Change control: new pages or integrations are quoted before work begins.

Checklist

  • Is the outcome written in the client’s words?
  • Does every input have an owner and a date?
  • Is each milestone a visible deliverable?
  • Is the communication rhythm agreed?
  • Are scope and change control restated in writing?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting production before the decision-maker is identified.
  • Leaving content, access, or approvals without owners.
  • Using vague milestones such as “work continues.”
  • Skipping the written kickoff summary.

FAQ

How long should a kickoff take?

Forty-five minutes is enough for a small project if you prepare the checklist. Complex projects may need a separate discovery workshop.

What if the client cannot join?

Reschedule or require a written answer to the checklist. Starting without the decision-maker creates avoidable rework.

Should I record the kickoff?

Only with consent. A written summary with owners and dates is usually more useful and easier to correct.
