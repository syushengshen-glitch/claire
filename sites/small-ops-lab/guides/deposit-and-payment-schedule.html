Deposit and payment schedule patterns for small projects

Choose a deposit, milestones, and final payment that protect both cash flow and trust.

Reviewed October 12, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Use a deposit that covers mobilization, tie later payments to visible milestones, keep a final payment before final handoff, and write the schedule into the quote.

The problem

A large deposit can scare away a good client, while no deposit can leave you financing the project. The right schedule matches the risk and the delivery timeline.

The deposit pays for starting, not for trust

A deposit covers the work required before the first deliverable: planning, scheduling, setup, research, or reserved capacity. Explain that reason in the quote instead of treating the deposit as a test of the client.

Match payments to visible progress

Think in milestones the client can see: approval of the direction, delivery of the first draft, completion of the build, or launch readiness. Avoid dates that are disconnected from a deliverable, because they turn into collection calls.

Keep the final payment before final handoff

For small projects, release source files, admin access, or the final export after the final payment clears. Say this before the project starts. Do not hold a client’s live business hostage over a disputed extra; separate the dispute from the handoff.

Handle long projects with a monthly rhythm

For retainers or multi-month work, bill on a schedule: monthly in advance, every two weeks, or per sprint. State what happens if work is paused by the client and whether unused hours roll over.

Write the schedule where the client will see it

Put the payment amounts, triggers, due dates, accepted methods, and late-payment terms in the quote and the contract. A payment schedule buried in an email will be forgotten.

Example

Three-payment schedule

40% deposit — reserves the build week and covers discovery.

30% at approved design direction — starts production.

30% before final handoff — releases source files, access, and launch support.

Checklist

  • Does the deposit cover the work before the first milestone?
  • Is every payment tied to a visible deliverable?
  • Is the final payment collected before final handoff?
  • Are paused projects and unused hours addressed?
  • Is the schedule in both the quote and the contract?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting work with no deposit because the client seems friendly.
  • Tying payments to calendar dates that do not match progress.
  • Delivering all files before the final payment clears.
  • Changing the schedule mid-project without updating the agreement.

FAQ

What deposit percentage is normal?

Small projects often use 30% to 50%. The right number depends on your setup costs, project length, and the client’s payment process.

Should I offer a discount for full prepayment?

Only if the discount is already priced into your numbers and your cash flow can handle the risk. Do not discount to avoid a payment conversation.

Can I charge interest on late payments?

Only where the law, contract, and client agreement allow it. Use a clear late fee or service pause instead of an unexpected penalty.
