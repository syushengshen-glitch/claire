Invoice follow-up sequence: day 1, day 7, day 14, day 30

A calm reminder system that protects the relationship and the cash flow.

Reviewed October 12, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Send a friendly confirmation when the invoice is issued, a neutral reminder after the due date, a firmer reminder with a new deadline, and a final notice that explains the next step.

The problem

Most overdue invoices are not a legal problem yet. They are an attention problem, a process problem, or a sign that the original payment terms were unclear.

Before the invoice: remove avoidable delays

Confirm the client’s legal entity, billing contact, purchase-order requirement, and preferred invoice format before the work starts. A perfect reminder sequence cannot fix an invoice sent to the wrong system.

Day 0: send the invoice with a clear reference

State the project name, invoice number, amount, due date, payment method, and the person to contact with a question. Attach the timesheet, milestone evidence, or deliverable reference so the client does not need to ask for proof.

Day 1 after due: assume a process delay

Use a short, neutral message: “The invoice was due yesterday. Has it reached the right queue, or do you need anything from me to process it?” This gives an administrative contact an easy way to help without escalating to a conflict.

Day 7: add a new decision date

Repeat the invoice details, state the new date, and ask who can confirm payment. If work is ongoing, state clearly whether it will pause if the invoice remains unpaid. Keep the wording factual and avoid sarcasm.

Day 14 and day 30: escalate the process, not the emotion

At day 14, copy the agreed late-payment policy and ask for a payment date in writing. At day 30, send a final notice that states the next step, such as pausing work, involving a collections process, or ending future delivery. Do not threaten anything you are not prepared to do.

Example

Day 7 reminder

Subject: Invoice 1042 — new payment date

Hi Maya, Invoice 1042 for $2,400 was due on October 5. I have not seen payment or a processing note yet. Could you confirm whether it is scheduled, and by what date? If the invoice needs a purchase order or a different billing contact, tell me and I will resend it today.

Checklist

  • Is the invoice sent to the correct billing system?
  • Does it include a PO or project reference?
  • Are the due date and payment method obvious?
  • Does the follow-up ask for a specific action?
  • Does the final notice match the written policy?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting a month before the first reminder.
  • Writing an angry message before checking the billing process.
  • Threatening collections you will not actually use.
  • Continuing new work while the account is overdue without a decision.

FAQ

How soon after the due date should I follow up?

One business day after the due date is reasonable. It catches administrative delays early and keeps the tone neutral.

Should I pause work when an invoice is late?

If the contract or policy allows it, pause new work after a clear warning. Continue only if there is a written payment commitment and the risk is acceptable.

Can I automate these reminders?

Yes, but keep the messages short and personal enough to answer a question. Automation should trigger the reminder, not remove the ability to respond.
