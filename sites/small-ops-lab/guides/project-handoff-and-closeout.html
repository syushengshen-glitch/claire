Project handoff and closeout: what to deliver and what to keep

A clean closeout protects access, payment, future work, and your reputation.

Reviewed October 12, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Use a closeout checklist: final payment, files and access, credentials transfer, documentation, support window, feedback, and future-work options.

The problem

A project is not finished when the last deliverable is approved. It is finished when access, files, payment, and support expectations are settled.

Confirm final payment and the ownership trigger

Check the contract for the exact moment ownership or final files transfer. If payment is outstanding, follow the agreed process instead of releasing everything and hoping to collect later.

Deliver the files in the agreed format

Provide the final source files, exports, documentation, and a short readme that explains what each file is. If the client expected editable files, confirm that they open and are usable, not just present.

Transfer access safely

Use a shared password manager or the platform’s transfer flow. Do not send credentials in plain text. Remove your own access only after the client confirms they can log in and the final payment is complete.

Define the support window

State what is covered after launch, for how long, and how new requests are handled. A 14-day bug-fix window plus a separate support plan prevents “quick questions” from becoming permanent free work.

Close the loop and ask for the next step

Send a short closeout summary with results, files, and support terms. Ask for feedback, a testimonial if appropriate, and one specific referral. Offer a maintenance or phase-two option with a clear scope.

Example

Closeout email skeleton

The site is live and the final invoice is paid. Attached: source files, export, credentials transfer note, and a two-page handoff guide.

Support: bug fixes are covered for 14 days. New features or content changes can be quoted separately.

If you have five minutes, a short testimonial would help. If a specific person comes to mind who needs the same fix, I am happy to send a one-paragraph introduction.

Checklist

  • Is the final payment confirmed?
  • Are files delivered in an openable, agreed format?
  • Was access transferred without plain-text credentials?
  • Is the support window written down?
  • Did I ask for feedback and a specific next step?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Releasing all files before final payment clears.
  • Sending passwords in an email or chat message.
  • Leaving support expectations undefined.
  • Ending the project with no feedback or future-work conversation.

FAQ

Should I keep a copy of the final files?

Keep only what the contract and privacy rules allow. If you keep files for future work, say how long they are stored and how they are protected.

What if the client asks for extra work after launch?

Treat it as a change request or a new project. Offer a small support plan, a fixed-fee task, or a phase-two quote.

Is a testimonial request appropriate?

Yes, after a successful closeout. Ask for specific feedback and make it easy to decline.
