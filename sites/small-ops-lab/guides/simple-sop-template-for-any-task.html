A simple SOP template for any repeated task

Write a process someone else can run without asking you five questions.

Reviewed October 12, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Use a one-page SOP with seven fields: purpose, trigger, owner, inputs, steps, exceptions, and done. Test it on a real task and revise the page where the runner gets stuck.

The problem

Most small teams do not need a complex process wiki. They need one page that states the trigger, owner, inputs, steps, exceptions, and definition of done.

Start with the trigger

A process starts when something happens: a signed quote, a new support ticket, a monthly date, or a failed payment. If the trigger is vague, the process will be skipped. Write the exact event and how the owner is notified.

Name one owner, not a team

A team cannot be accountable. Name the person responsible for running the process, the person who approves an exception, and the backup. If no one owns the process, it will quietly disappear.

Separate inputs from steps

Inputs are the information, access, or files required before the first step. Steps are the actions. Keeping them separate makes it obvious when a process is blocked by missing information rather than a broken procedure.

Write the exceptions before they happen

List the top five situations where the normal path does not apply: a refund, a missing approval, a data error, a VIP client, or a system outage. For each one, state what the owner should do and who to notify.

Define done and schedule a review

“Done” should be observable: the invoice is logged, the client receives the file, the ticket is closed, the backup is verified. Add a review date and an owner for updates. An SOP without a review date becomes fiction.

Example

One-page SOP fields

Purpose: send the monthly client report.

Trigger: the first business day of the month.

Owner: operations lead; backup: account manager.

Inputs: analytics export, project notes, open issues.

Steps: validate data, draft summary, review numbers, send, log delivery.

Exceptions: missing analytics access, client request to pause, data discrepancy.

Done: report delivered and logged; client question assigned to an owner.

Checklist

  • Is the trigger a specific event?
  • Does one person own the process?
  • Are inputs listed separately from steps?
  • Are the top exceptions covered?
  • Is “done” observable and reviewable?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Writing a long policy instead of a runnable procedure.
  • Assigning the process to a team with no owner.
  • Leaving exceptions to the runner’s judgment.
  • Publishing the SOP without testing it on a real task.

FAQ

How long should an SOP be?

One page is enough for most small tasks. If it is longer, split it into separate processes with their own triggers.

What tool should I use?

A document, a shared note, or a task template can work. The tool matters less than the trigger, owner, and definition of done.

How often should I review it?

Review quarterly or after a failure, a team change, or a tool migration. Assign the review to a named owner.
