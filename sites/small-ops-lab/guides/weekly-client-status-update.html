Weekly client status update that reduces surprise meetings

A short, repeatable update that makes progress, blockers, and decisions visible.

Reviewed October 12, 2026 · Practical operations guide · Advertising is labeled separately

Quick answer: Send a five-part update every week: progress since last week, next week, blockers, decisions needed, and budget or timeline status.

The problem

Clients ask for more meetings when they cannot see progress, blockers, or the next decision. A predictable written update solves most of that problem.

Lead with the status, not the story

Start with one line: on track, at risk, or blocked. Clients should not have to read three paragraphs to discover that a deadline moved. If the status changed, explain the cause and the recovery plan.

Report progress the client can verify

Link to the deliverable, the task board, or the approval. “Worked on the site” is not a status. “Homepage draft is ready for review; booking flow is next” is verifiable.

Separate blockers from work in progress

List anything waiting on the client, a vendor, or a decision. Add the owner and the impact if the blocker is not resolved by a date. Do not hide a blocker to look efficient; it will surface later with more cost.

Make the decision request explicit

Ask for one decision at a time, with a recommended option and a deadline. Attach the evidence needed to decide. If no decision is needed, say “no decision needed this week,” which reduces unnecessary replies.

Keep budget and timeline visible

Add a simple line for time or budget used and the forecast to completion. A small variance early is easier to discuss than a large overrun at the end. If the forecast changed, explain what changed it.

Example

Weekly update template

Status: on track.

Done: sitemap approved; homepage draft ready for review.

Next: booking page build; need final copy by Wednesday.

Blockers: waiting on logo files (Sam, due Tuesday).

Decisions: choose booking provider A or B by Thursday — recommendation is A for lower setup cost.

Budget: 18 of 40 hours used; forecast remains 40 hours.

Checklist

  • Does the first line state on track, at risk, or blocked?
  • Can the client verify the progress through a link or deliverable?
  • Does every blocker have an owner and a date?
  • Is the decision request specific and recommended?
  • Are budget and timeline visible?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Sending an update only when something goes wrong.
  • Burying a blocker in the middle of a long message.
  • Asking for three decisions in one paragraph.
  • Reporting hours without a forecast.

FAQ

How long should the update be?

Aim for a five-minute read: one status line, short bullets, and one clear decision request. Long updates are usually a sign that the project plan is unclear.

Should I send updates if nothing changed?

Yes, but keep it short. “No change; next milestone remains Friday” prevents the client from wondering whether work stopped.

What if the client never reads the update?

Ask which format and channel work for them, and confirm the decision deadline in a separate short message when a response is required.
